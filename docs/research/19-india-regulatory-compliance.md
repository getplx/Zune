## 1. Summary & recommendation

India is workable for a 200-family, parent-contracted, internet-only pilot, but the binding risks differ from reports 11-13.

1. **Telecom licensing is not the main risk** (**[CORRECTED]** [INFERRED] only: the 2026 DoT authorisation, radio-equipment possession and user-identification rules were not read, see 2.2 and question 11). D19 removes PSTN, SMS and carrier-of-record issues. The live issues are DPDP s.9 (children), IT Act intermediary duties, POCSO reporting and a policy push against minors on messengers.
2. **A parental-control vendor has no class exemption.** DPDP s.9(3) bans "tracking or behavioural monitoring of children". The Fourth Schedule exempts clinics, schools, crèches and school transport (Part A), and purposes such as child-safety location and keeping harmful content from the child (Part B) [PRIMARY]. Parents reading messages, call metadata and AI chats is **not squarely exempt**. The defensible reading: the parent is part of the "Data Principal" for a child (s.2(j)(i)) and consents, so Zune is a conduit, not a monitor. Unsettled (gate G1).
3. **Dates.** s.9 and Rule 10 bind from **13 May 2027**; MeitY floated 13 Nov 2026 (not gazetted as far as I could see) [SECONDARY]. Behave as if the law is in force now.
4. **Direction-of-travel risk.** On 28 Sep 2026 the Centre told the Supreme Court it will amend the IT Rules so platforms cannot let under-18s open social-media accounts [SECONDARY; **[CORRECTED]** UNVERIFIED in the second pass: source blocked, no mirror found; see section 2.4 and the Verification section]. A messenger is plausibly a "social media intermediary". Position Zune as a **family-managed, closed-contact tool** (no discovery, handles, feeds or groups).
5. **Recommendation.** Invite-only, parent-contracted pilot through an Indian entity with India-region hosting, Rule-10 parent verification at the provisioning visit, purpose-limited monitoring, a 112 path plus triple-press SOS, and counsel sign-off (section 4.4) before any external family is invited or charged.

- **Roles.** Zune is the Data Fiduciary. AI, cloud, push, media and SMS vendors are Data Processors under contract (s.8(2)); we stay liable (s.8(1)). A parent's own reading is outside the Act as "personal or domestic purpose" (s.3(c)(i)); our processing is not.
- **Children.** Child = under 18 (s.2(f)), so age bands do not reduce the burden. s.9(1): verifiable parental consent before any processing. Rule 10: the parent must be an identifiable adult via details we hold, or details or a **virtual token from an authorised entity, including DigiLocker**. s.9(2) bars processing likely to harm well-being. **[CORRECTED]** Rule 10(1) is looser than "authorised entity only" and the report overstated it: limb (a) is "reliable details of identity and age ... available with the Data Fiduciary"; limb (b)(i) is details "voluntarily provided by the individual", and only (b)(ii) is a virtual token "issued by an authorised entity"; the "identifiable" element applies only "if required in connection with compliance with any law for the time being in force in India" [PRIMARY: Rules mirrors, Rule 10(1)]. But Illustrations Cases 2 and 4 send a parent who is not yet a registered user to details issued by an entity entrusted by law or Government, or a virtual token, so a staff-inspected government ID plus a stored record (Cases 1 and 3 for later children) is defensible, not certain: keep counsel question 2. That a card payment proves adulthood is not stated anywhere; the Rule names no such method, and a card cannot evidence age [INFERRED].
- **Exemptions (Rule 12, Fourth Schedule).** Part B: (4) real-time location "in the interest of safety, protection or security"; (5) processing "to the extent necessary" to keep harmful information, services or ads from the child; (6) age and parent verification. **No row for parental control or messaging.** The s.17(3) startup carve-out never covers s.9 [PRIMARY: it lists only s.5, s.8(3), s.8(7), ss.10 and 11]. **[CORRECTED]** (a) Rule 12 lifts only s.9(1) and s.9(3), never s.9(2) (no processing likely to harm a child's well-being), which bites on the AI assistant and the messenger; (b) Part B(3) exempts a child account "limited to communication by email", which a messenger is not; (c) the report omitted **s.9(5)**: the Central Government may, for a fiduciary whose processing of children's data is "verifiably safe", notify an **age above which** s.9(1) and (3) stop applying to it. That is the statutory route closest to this product (a safe-by-design kids OS for 13-17s) and belongs beside s.9(4) and s.17(5) in the MeitY dialogue and counsel question 1 [PRIMARY: Act s.9(4)-(5), s.17(5)].
- **Cross-border and scale.** Transfer allowed unless a country is notified (s.16); Rule 13(4) localises only data specified for Significant Data Fiduciaries. **No blanket localisation.** Significant status turns on volume, sensitivity and risk (s.10), not a user count; a children's platform is a plausible future candidate [INFERRED].
- **Breach and retention.** Detailed report to the Board within **72 hours**, plus notice to each affected principal (Rule 7). Rule 6: encryption, access control, logs, one-year log retention. **Rule 8(3): keep personal data, traffic data and processing logs at least one year** (State-access purposes).
- **Process and penalties.** 90-day grievance route (Rule 14); published contact person (Rule 9); withdrawal as easy as consent (s.6(4)); notices must offer "English or any language specified in the Eighth Schedule" (ss.5(3), 6(3)). **[CORRECTED]** the text gives the Data Principal "the option to access" the notice or consent request "in English or any language specified in the Eighth Schedule" [PRIMARY: Act ss.5(3), 6(3)]: the principal chooses, so an English-only product does not clearly satisfy it, and section 6's "English alone arguably satisfies" is too optimistic. Treat translated parent notices (at least Hindi, on request for the other Eighth Schedule languages) as a legal need, not a product-language feature, and get the founder's explicit exception to D20 for that purpose (decision 3). Penalties: Rs 200 crore for s.9, Rs 250 crore for security, Rs 200 crore for breach notification.
- **Status.** Board exists in law. **[CORRECTED]** the earlier line "chair reported appointed June 2026" is unsupported and contradicted: The Breakdown (15 Jun 2026) says the chairperson and members "have yet to be appointed", and a LiveLaw piece of 1 Aug 2026 ("Established In Law, Absent In Fact", as cited in technology-ashiq/arc ADR 1206) reports no Chairperson or Members as of 1 Aug 2026 [SECONDARY, two independent sources; nothing later found]. No enforcement body is seated, which does not change the design duties. ### 2.2 Telecom and messaging
- **Broad text, untested.** "Telecommunication" is transmission of messages by wire, radio, optical or other electromagnetic systems; a service for it needs authorisation; operating without one is an offence (up to three years or Rs 2 crore) (ss.2(p), (t), 3(1)(a), 42(1)) [PRIMARY: abhinandansethi/TMT-Tracker@HEAD:data/act_text/telecom_2023.json]. TRAI (Sept 2024) excluded OTT from authorisation, and DoT created a separate "Telecommunication Identifier User Entity" (TIUE) category [SECONDARY] https://bestmediainfo.com/mediainfo/ott/trai-suggests-service-authorisations-framework-excludes-ott-platforms-from-licensing-regime-7077869 . Likely not an authorisable service, risk not zero [INFERRED]. **[CORRECTED]** this conclusion rests on a Sept 2024 TRAI recommendation and a blocked link, and it **predates the 2026 authorisation rules the report never mentions**: DoT's eServices shelf lists *Authorisation for Provision of Principal Telecommunication Services Rules, 2026* and *Authorisation for Provision of Miscellaneous Telecommunication Services Rules, 2026* (both 23 Jun 2026), *Telecommunications (Authorisation for Telecommunication Network) Rules, 2026* (in force 20 Jul 2026), and *Telecommunications (Radio Equipment Possession Authorisation) Rules, 2026* (in force 8 Jul 2026) [SECONDARY: abhinandansethi/TMT-Tracker@HEAD:data/rules_shelf.json and data/items.json; **texts not read**, egress blocked]. Whether in-app voice/video or a messenger falls in "miscellaneous" services, and whether a company that holds customers' radio-equipped phones for flashing (s.3(1)(c) "possess radio equipment") needs or is exempt from an authorisation, are open and now **counsel question 11**. The statement that licensing "is not the main risk" is therefore [INFERRED], not established.
- **TIUE and SIM binding.** Amendment Rules (22 Oct 2025) make any non-licensee that uses telecom identifiers (mobile numbers) to identify users a TIUE [SECONDARY] https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/10/25/telecommunications-telecom-cyber-security-amendment-rules-2025-legal-news/ . The 28 Nov 2025 DoT direction keeps number-based messengers bound to the device SIM; deadline extended to **31 Dec 2026** [SECONDARY] https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/dot-extends-sim-binding-deadline-for-messaging-apps-to-december-31 . In July 2026 the Government sent notices to WhatsApp, Telegram and Signal over **phone-number-free usernames** [SECONDARY] https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/whatsapp-submits-reply-to-govt-notice-on-username-feature-542137-2026-07-10 . A number-free kid messenger is policy-adverse unless each child traces to a verified adult; a mobile-OTP parent portal probably makes us a TIUE [INFERRED]. **[CORRECTED]** (a) the Nov 2025 direction and the TIUE definition are corroborated: DoT told app-based communication services that use an Indian mobile number to identify users (WhatsApp, Telegram, Snapchat, Arattai, ShareChat, Josh, JioChat, Signal) to keep the app continuously bound to the active SIM and log web sessions out every six hours, within 90 days; it amends the Telecom Cyber Security Rules 2024 [SECONDARY: The Hacker News, 2 Dec 2025, via mthcht/ThreatIntel-Reports]. The **31 Dec 2026 extension and the July 2026 username notices are UNVERIFIED** (Tribune and BusinessToday blocked, no mirror found); (b) the direction targets messaging *apps* keyed on a number, so a browser parent portal that merely uses OTP login being a TIUE is a stretch; the child's messenger, which has no number, is the real exposure; (c) **new and omitted:** *Telecommunications (User Identification) Rules, 2026*, gazetted 21 Aug 2026 under s.56 (e-Gazette ID CG-DL-E-21082026-275657; one source says 22 Aug) and in force on publication: biometric-backed e-KYC or live-face D-KYC for new SIMs, three months to comply, extendable to six; a DoT circular reported 20 Aug 2026 caps new SIMs at nine per subscriber [SECONDARY: TMT-Tracker items and signals; amehta0104/ditto-research-public@HEAD:research/2026-08-27-cycle-delta.md citing biometricupdate.com; texts not read]. It governs SIM issuance to the parent, not our product, but it makes "buy a data SIM for the child" an ordinary adult-KYC'd purchase (see 4.1).
- **Interception.** Telecom Act s.20(2) lets the Government order interception or suspension; **s.42(2)(b) makes unlawful interception an offence**; offences are cognizable and non-bailable (s.42(7)) [PRIMARY]. IT Act s.69 and the 2009 Interception Rules bind intermediaries [MEMORY]; **[CORRECTED]** DoT's *Procedures and Safeguards for Lawful Interception of Messages Rules, 2024* also exist and were not considered [SECONDARY: TMT-Tracker data/rules_shelf.json; text not read]. Zune is the server endpoint, not a third-party interceptor, but disclosure to the other child's parent needs counsel.
- **Mandates.** The Sanchar Saathi pre-install order was withdrawn 3 Dec 2025 [SECONDARY] https://techobserver.in/news/egov/govt-withdraws-order-mandating-pre-installed-sanchar-saathi-on-all-phones-319314/ . Smartphone ITSAR (83 requirements): a Jan 2026 source-code proposal was denied by MeitY and I found no notification [SECONDARY] https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/12/india_mobile_security/ . It would bind handset makers; whether a reflasher counts is open [INFERRED].
- **Handset duties.** Panic Button and GPS Rules 2016: smartphones must trigger a panic call on three quick power-key presses and have GPS, binding makers and importers [SECONDARY] https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?relid=142272&reg=48&lang=2 ; old rules survive under Telecom Act s.61 [PRIMARY]. **Replacing the OS removes the vendor's compliant SOS unless we rebuild it.** 112 (ERSS) has run nationally since 2019; 1098 Childline is being merged into it [SECONDARY] **[CORRECTED]** the 112 roll-out was phased from 2019 [MEMORY], and report 20's pass found the 1098 merge is phased state by state with 1098 still working, so do not print "1098 is part of 112" until Karnataka's status is confirmed; **the "Panic Button and GPS Rules 2016" name, scope and effective dates were not read** (the PIB link was never opened; my searches found no text): from memory the 2016 rules made the three-press panic call mandatory for smartphones from 1 Jan 2017 and GPS from 1 Jan 2018, enforced on makers and importers [MEMORY, UNVERIFIED]. ## 4. Recommended Stage-1 design

### 4.1 Architecture
- **Entity and hosting:** Indian private limited; AWS Mumbai primary, Hyderabad DR; Indian NTP; 180-day logs in India.
- **Parent identity:** one verified adult per family (DigiLocker token, or staff-inspected government ID at the D16 visit), mobile OTP, consent ledger. Child accounts exist only under a verified parent; the device never shows the child an "I agree".
- **Monitoring:** parent-directed and purpose-bound (safety, content gating); no analytics SDKs, ad IDs or behavioural profiles; video topic search uses the current question only. Location only on SOS (Part B(4)); weather uses a parent-set city.
- **Both-family consent** per contact pair, naming what each parent sees (report 13).
- **Retention:** message content, AI transcripts and logs **12 months** in a restricted vault, then deleted, until counsel narrows Rule 8(3). Vendors keep nothing.
