## 1. Summary & recommendation

D15 (sell the image) and D16 (v1 is company-flashed; customer self-install later) are workable, but the blob problem moves rather than disappears. A phone relocked with our AVB key accepts only payloads we sign, so firmware, modem and vendor patches must ship inside **our** OTA. That needs a right to redistribute OEM bytes, not just resell hardware. D16 buys a legal runway: in v1 we flash the customer's own phone with firmware fetched from the OEM, a lighter posture than publishing a download [INFERRED, counsel]. The question returns with the first monthly firmware update.

1. **One installer, used first as our provisioning station (D16), released to customers in v2.** Web-based (WebUSB, fastboot.js, MIT) with a stricter pre-flight than GrapheneOS's. Station operations belong to report 18.
2. **Relock with our AVB key is mandatory for every "Zune Ready" device** (A7), verified by attestation at the station (v1) and gating self-install claims (v2). No unlocked tier.
3. **Devices (reconciling 15):** Pixel 9a/10a (`zumapro`) first for engineering and private beta (documented relock, adevtool). Fairphone Gen 6+ (15's pick) becomes the commercial launch if it signs written firmware-redistribution terms and passes the relock test. The gate is OEM terms plus verified relock, not the SoC.
4. **Money:** a one-time provisioning fee (v1 labour, shipping, QA) plus a **per-child subscription**; the image is useless without an account, so cloud services are the paywall. A one-time-only image fee is a trap: AI, relay, patch and support costs recur, and EU update duties outlast it.
5. **Compliance relief is partial.** No equipment authorisation or inventory, but under D16 we hold customers' phones: custody, wipe, warranty and CPSIA framing need counsel. Keep the legal form an installation service on a customer-owned phone; never sell the handset.

### F5. Installer facts
- GrapheneOS's installer is `fastboot.js` (MIT) in the browser: `product` in a 21-codename allowlist, `unlocked` check, `flashing unlock`, download to browser storage, `snapshot-update:cancel`, `flashFactoryZip` (firmware, reboot, OS, `avb_custom_key`), `flashing lock`; setup then offers to disable OEM unlocking [PRIMARY: GrapheneOS/grapheneos.org@main:static/js/web-install.js]. Its pre-flight is thin.
- Documented failure modes: 2 GB RAM, 32 GB disk; Chromium only (no Snap/Flatpak, no private mode); Windows driver; Linux udev and `fwupd`; hubs and cables ("most common source of issues"); no VMs; carrier variants block unlock; the unlock toggle needs internet; it can run from an Android phone [PRIMARY: static/install/web.html]. Firefox and Safari lack WebUSB [SECONDARY]. **[CORRECTED]** The same page says current Windows 10/11 need no separate fastboot driver (only outdated Windows does), so "drivers" is a lesser failure cause than cables/hubs, which it calls "the most common source of issues". Its Android-host option requires Android 14-17 "with Play Protect certification" (or GrapheneOS/ChromeOS), which a Zune handset can never be, so a parent needs a different phone. Its supported-device allowlist has exactly 21 codenames including `tegu` (9a) and `stallion` (10a); adevtool@17 has `stallion.yml` (US ODM SKU GE1GQ) and `tegu.yml` (US SKU GXQ96) [PRIMARY]. The JS itself only issues `flashing unlock`, `snapshot-update:cancel`, `flashFactoryZip` (the `avb_custom_key` step lives in the vendored fastboot.js flow and the factory zip, labelled "verified boot key" in the UI), `flashing lock` and `erase:avb_custom_key`.
- Standard fastboot variables are only `version`, `version-bootloader`, `version-baseband`, `product`, `serialno`, `secure`, `is-userspace`, `is-logical`; everything else is OEM-specific, so each device needs an adapter [PRIMARY: LineageOS/android_system_core@lineage-24.0:fastboot/README.md]. **[CORRECTED]** The README does list only those eight (version, version-bootloader, version-baseband, product, serialno, secure, is-userspace, plus `is-logical:` in its Logical Partitions section), but it is stale. The same tree's fastbootd (`fastboot/device/commands.cpp`, AOSP code) defines 38 variables in its `kVariableMap`, including `unlocked`, `current-slot`, `slot-count`, `has-slot`, `slot-successful`, `slot-unbootable`, `max-download-size`, `partition-size`, `snapshot-update-status`, `hw-revision`, `security-patch-level`, `first-api-level`, `battery-voltage`, `battery-soc-ok`, and the host `fastboot` client calls several of them. So "unlocked, slots, battery are OEM-specific" is wrong for fastbootd. The conclusion survives for a different reason: unlock, lock and `avb_custom_key` are done in the OEM bootloader's fastboot, which is OEM-implemented and varies (carrier id is genuinely OEM-specific; Fairphone needs a two-step unlock and a per-device code), so per-device adapters are still needed. Source is the LineageOS mirror, not Google's tree. The Updater and adevtool are MIT [PRIMARY]. Losing the AVB key forces unlock and wipe on every device [PRIMARY: avbroot README].

### F6. Attestation
RootOfTrust carries `verifiedBootKey`, `deviceLocked`, `verifiedBootState` [MEMORY; schema page blocked]. A new ECDSA root signs chains from 2026-02-01; trust both roots [PRIMARY: developer.android.com/privacy-and-security/security-key-attestation]. Recent devices fetch attestation keys from Google's remote provisioning; GrapheneOS proxies it, and a non-Google OS "would need their own service" [PRIMARY: FAQ].
**[CORRECTED] (four points, all second pass).**
- *Schema now PRIMARY.* `verified_boot_key`, `device_locked` and `VerifiedBoot::SELF_SIGNED = 1` are in GrapheneOS/platform_hardware_interfaces@17:security/keymint/support/include/keymint_support/attestation_record.h; `Tag::ROOT_OF_TRUST` is documented as "the boot key, verified boot state, boot hash, and whether device is locked" (Tag.aidl). This is a GrapheneOS fork of AOSP, not Google's tree.
- *New root confirmed.* The developer.android.com page (last updated 2026-07-09) still says in the future tense that the new root "will begin signing attestation certificate chains on February 1, 2026". Decoded: "Key Attestation CA1", ECDSA P-384 with SHA-384, valid 2025-07-17 to 2035-07-15 [PRIMARY]. Verifier code must trust both roots.
- *RKP claim misapplied.* The FAQ sentence is about "a device built to run another OS without Google certification". For a retail phone running our image, the FAQ shows GrapheneOS reaching Google's service through a proxy to `remoteprovisioning.googleapis.com`, so the BYO path can use Google's service (report 05: the rkpd host is a system property); what remains open is Google's terms and whether it keeps serving non-certified OSes [INFERRED]. "A non-Google OS needs its own" is overstated for BYO and right only for our own future hardware.
- *Attestation is relay-bypassable.* GrapheneOS's own install page warns the first verification "could" be bypassed "with a leaked attestation key or by proxying to another device with the device model, OS and patch level that the user is expecting", and says hardware serial-number attestation is the planned fix [PRIMARY: static/install/web.html]. So section 4.1 step 5 ("gate the claim on attestation") and section 4.4 ("one seat per attested device key") do not stop one genuine device attesting on behalf of many pirated installs. Bind the seat to ID attestation of the serial recorded at the station (a privileged system app can request it [MEMORY]) plus per-device mTLS and server-side anomaly checks. The same page says installing GrapheneOS "flashes the GrapheneOS verified boot public key to the secure element" on Pixels, consistent with the key being unrotatable without an unlock and wipe (README says `avb_custom_key` is writable only while unlocked).

### F7. Law (all [SECONDARY] search summaries unless noted; counsel to confirm)
- **FCC:** software changes that do not affect RF need no new filing; a third party altering RF behaviour becomes responsible. We keep modem and Wi-Fi firmware untouched. **[CORRECTED]** The FCC's software-change guidance (KDB 178919, quoted in search summaries of 47 CFR 2.1043; eCFR itself blocked) is stricter than "becomes responsible": third-party activation of software changes to any radio parameter (frequency, output power, modulation, operating circumstances) is *not allowed* unless the device was approved as a software-defined radio, and "third party" expressly includes operating-system providers. Non-RF software changes may be made by non-grantees without filing. So the rule for us is: change no RF-relevant parameter at all, including Wi-Fi regulatory-domain and TX-power handling and carrier configuration shipped in our system image [SECONDARY, counsel]. A 2026 FCC equipment-authorization rulemaking on "covered equipment" turned up in search but concerns national-security-listed equipment, not Pixel or Fairphone [SECONDARY].
- **CPSIA:** "children's product" = a "consumer product" (an "article") designed or intended primarily for children 12 or under; no CPSC guidance found on software or general-use phones marketed to kids. Risk rises if we hand back a phone marketed as a kids' product.
- **Liability:** Magnuson-Moss bars voiding a warranty for third-party software unless it caused the defect. UK CRA 2015 s.46: digital content that damages a consumer's device through lack of reasonable care means repair or compensation. EU Directive 2019/770: updates for the period a consumer may reasonably expect. EU CRA reporting is live since 2026-09-11 (rest 2027-12-11); Product Liability Directive 2024/2853 covers software placed on the market after 2026-12-09, including missing security updates. **[CORRECTED]** Re-checked via search (Commission and law-firm pages; EUR-Lex blocked): CRA reporting duties (24 h early warning, 72 h notification) apply from 2026-09-11 and the main CRA obligations from 2027-12-11 [SECONDARY, confirmed]; the PLD must be transposed by 2026-12-09 and applies to products placed on the market after that date [SECONDARY, confirmed]; UK Consumer Rights Act 2015 s.46 is "Remedy for damage to device or to other digital content" (repair or compensate where lack of reasonable care and skill damaged the device) [SECONDARY, confirmed]. The PLD does not itself create an update duty; it conditions liability on manufacturer control and failure to act, so "including missing security updates" is a reading, not a rule [SECONDARY].
- **Unlocking:** the DMCA s.1201 jailbreak exemption (renewed Oct 2024) covers the act, not trafficking in tools; OEM-sanctioned unlock is not circumvention. **Sanctioned paths only; never exploit-based unlocks (EDL, Qualcomm GBL)** [INFERRED, counsel]. **[CORRECTED]** The ninth-triennial final rule was issued 2024-10-25 and effective 2024-10-28; the jailbreak exemptions run to October 2027 and must be renewed in the next triennial, so D16's legal footing needs a 2027 re-check [SECONDARY]. The 2015 wording of the smartphone exemption, as I recall it, covers circumvention initiated by the owner or by another person at the owner's direction, which would fit D16's company flashing [MEMORY, not re-read; Federal Register blocked].
- **Sales:** FTC click-to-cancel vacated July 2025; California's amended ARL (2025-07-01) requires online cancellation and annual reminders. Merchants of record (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy) charge about 5% + $0.50 and handle VAT and sales tax. Platform-TLS apps are likely ECCN 5D992.c or EAR99. Google bars "Android" in product names; say "built on the Android Open Source Project" [SECONDARY]. **[CORRECTED / added, second pass, all [SECONDARY] search summaries, counsel to confirm]:**
- *FTC:* click-to-cancel was vacated by the Eighth Circuit on 2025-07-08; the FTC then restarted with an ANPRM published 2026-03-13 (comments closed 2026-04-13), so there is no final rule yet, and ROSCA and state laws still apply.
- *Merchant of record:* Paddle's 5% + $0.50 is all-in; Lemon Squeezy adds 1.5% international, 1.5% PayPal and 0.5% subscription surcharges, so "about 5% + $0.50" holds for Paddle only. On a $12.99 monthly charge the fixed $0.50 alone is 3.8%, so the effective take is nearer 9%.
- *Verizon:* the FCC granted Verizon a waiver in January 2026; phones must be paid off, and prepaid phones unlock after one year, with devices activated before 2026-01-20 grandfathered under the old 60-day rule (matches the report).
- *COPPA:* the amended Rule took effect 2025-06-23 and its compliance date was 2026-04-22, so it is already binding. It adds separate verifiable parental consent for non-integral third-party disclosure, a written retention policy and a written security programme. A card or other payment charge that notifies the account holder is a listed verifiable-consent method. Sending a child's images to an AI vendor (D11) is the obvious third-party-disclosure case. The report's "[MEMORY, counsel]" note on card-as-consent should be read as current law, not future.
- *State OS age-signal laws (omitted from the report):* California AB 1043 (Digital Age Assurance Act, signed 2025-10-13, effective 2027-01-01) requires an "operating system provider" to collect a birth date or age at account setup and expose a real-time age-bracket API (under 13, 13-15, 16-17, 18+) to apps in a covered application store; existing devices must get the interface before 2027-07-01. Zune ships an OS image, so it plausibly counts as an OS provider; whether a store-less OS is in scope is a counsel question. Colorado SB26-051 is a similar bill (status unverified). Texas's App Store Accountability Act is app-store-facing and was allowed to take effect after the Supreme Court declined to block it on 2026-07-06; it is likely out of scope for a store-less OS.
- *CPSIA:* the statutory definition is confirmed ("children's product" = a consumer product designed or intended primarily for children 12 or younger; "consumer product" = any article produced or distributed for sale to a consumer), and the factors include the manufacturer's own statement of intended use and whether consumers recognise it as a children's product, which Zune's marketing will supply.

**[CORRECTED]** Option (b)'s "Strongest" legal rating and "Our partitions only" are [INFERRED], not evidenced. The Google licence says the images may not be "modified or redistributed ... or used in any way except as specifically set forth in the license terms that came with your device" (enclosed text unread). Also, `boot`/`init_boot` (and probably `vendor_boot` and `dtbo`) must match our Android 17 system, so the genuinely "unmodified OEM bytes" are mainly bootloader, radio and secure-world firmware plus vendor/odm images re-wrapped in our vbmeta; the licence question covers that re-wrapping too. A variant the report does not consider is "fetch at update time": our updater downloads OEM firmware from the OEM's own server, hash-pinned in our signed manifest, so Zune never hosts OEM bytes after v1. That changes who redistributes but not whether the licence permits use; it is a counsel question, not a workaround.

## 4. Recommended Stage-1 design

**Stage-1 MVP:** a station-mode web installer for one Pixel platform (9a/10a) with strict pre-flight, relock and attestation check; manual qualification against the DSC; our OTA server (staged rollout, health gating); licence and subscription through the parent portal with a merchant of record; written OEM outreach running in parallel. Self-install, extra devices and OTA-delivered firmware wait for Stage 2 or gate G5.

### 4.1 Provisioning station (v1) and installer
Intake and operations are report 18's; the technical flow:
1. **Eligibility:** model and SKU lookup; US unlocked units only (carriers whitelist IMEI ranges of locally sold SKUs [PRIMARY: GrapheneOS usage.html]; **[CORRECTED]** the page says "some carriers may restrict functionality, such as VoLTE, on imported Pixel devices" because they "only whitelist the IMEI ranges of Pixel device SKUs which were sold locally", so it is a per-carrier risk for imports, not a universal rule; the same page also warns that Wi-Fi calling "may vary due to a reliance on proprietary Google apps", that some carriers provision VoLTE/VoWi-Fi through Google Firebase (GSMA TS.43; "very rare"), and that AT&T visual voicemail is unavailable without AOSP support, all of which bear on a no-GMS image and belong in report 12's carrier tests); owner requests any OEM unlock code; signed consent (two wipes, warranty, custody; accounts and screen lock removed).

3. Unlock (wipe), flash firmware, reboot, OS, `avb_custom_key`, lock (wipe), boot. Release signed (Ed25519) and hash-checked.
