## 1. Summary & recommendation

Run v1 as an **in-person-first, scripted service behind a hard eligibility gate**, not a mini-factory. Flashing is easy: about 10 unattended minutes plus two button presses. What hurts a pilot is the rest: phones that cannot be unlocked, carrier and account locks, stock firmware newer than our image, SIM handover, parent enrolment, and the terms for holding someone's phone.

1. **Gate before anything ships:** qualified model (15), unlocked and paid off, accounts removed, OEM unlocking available, not stolen. Customers self-test with screenshots.
2. **Cohorts of 10 (staff families), 25, then up to 100**, four weeks each, with exit gates. Batch size is still open (A7); these are defaults.
3. **Stations hold no secrets**, only the public key blob and signed bundles. Rotating the AVB key wipes every phone [P], so per-model keys and an offline signing ceremony come before cohort 2.
4. **Plan for the day after lock.** With OEM unlocking off (04, 05) a locked phone cannot be re-flashed over USB; OTA and signed recovery sideload are the only repair path. OTA ships from the first external device, and a parent-authorised **service-unlock** (17 proposes it) is required before cohort 2.
5. **Cleanest intake is a new-in-box phone** the parent buys and drops off: no data, accounts, FRP or carrier lock. Used phones are the gated exception. Few families own a qualified phone (15, 17), so recruitment is the real limit.
6. **Cost and price.** Fully loaded cost is $85-125 per device, not 17's $25-40 (station labour only). Charge $59 in person (17's default) and $99 mail-in; subsidy under $4k across 100 devices.
7. **Build self-install later**, at 250+ devices a month for two months or over 3 technician FTE; remote-guided install is the bridge.

## 2. Findings

**F1. Unlock and lock need a human at the phone.** `fastboot flashing unlock` and `lock` "need to be confirmed on the device and will wipe all data". OEM unlocking must be switched on inside the stock OS; on carrier SKUs that needs internet so the OS can check whether a carrier sold it locked [P: GrapheneOS/grapheneos.org@main:static/install/cli.html]. Two presses per phone cannot be scripted. **[CORRECTED]** Reproduced for Pixel: both commands "need to be confirmed on the device and will wipe all data" [P: GrapheneOS static/install/cli.html], and AVB requires physical presence and a data wipe for every LOCKED/UNLOCKED transition [P: LineageOS/android_external_avb@lineage-24.0:README.md, "Locked and Unlocked mode"]. "Two presses" is Pixel-only: Fairphone adds `flashing unlock_critical` and, on some software, an unlock code (17, 15 [S]). AOSP fastboot also has `flashing get_unlock_ability` ("unlocking is allowed (1) or not (0)") [P: GrapheneOS/platform_system_core@17:fastboot/fastboot.cpp usage text; upstream code], which the station should read in bootloader mode before the first press, and before any relock on non-Pixel models (15: relocking a Fairphone with ability 0 can brick it [S]).

**F2. Carrier variants are the biggest trap.** A carrier id on the Pixel `persist` partition disables unlocking, and carrier support "probably won't" lift it [P: same page]. The FCC waived Verizon's 60-day auto-unlock in 2026: payoff required, subsidised phones may stay locked up to a year; AT&T needs payoff plus 60 days, T-Mobile 40 days plus payoff [S: https://phandroid.com/2026/01/14/fcc-lets-verizon-ditch-60-day-phone-unlock-rule/ ; https://www.t-mobile.com/responsibility/consumer-info/policies/sim-unlock-policy]. **[CORRECTED]** The two cited pages were blocked; reproduced through other search summaries (fierce-network.com, broadbandbreakfast.com, 5gstore.com, droid-life.com 2026-02-16 and AT&T's own post on X). Precision: Verizon's change is an FCC **waiver** granted in January 2026 (an order, not a repeal of the rule); postpaid phones unlock after payoff and subsidised ones may stay locked up to a year; prepaid after 365 days of service. AT&T: paid off, account current, not reported lost or stolen, 60 days active (postpaid) or 6 months (prepaid). T-Mobile: 40 days active plus payoff on financed or leased phones [S]. Also separate two gates the gate list blurs: a carrier **SIM-unlock** and an **OEM bootloader unlock** are different. The persist-partition carrier id and Google's carrier-lock check decide the second [P: cli.html], so intake must test the OEM-unlocking toggle (and `get_unlock_ability`) itself and never infer it from SIM-unlock status.

**F3. Few phones can be relocked.** AVB custom key: while LOCKED the bootloader boots images signed by the built-in or the custom key, shows a yellow screen, and `avb_custom_key` is writable only while unlocked [P: LineageOS/android_external_avb@lineage-24.0:README.md]. Reported to work: Pixel, Fairphone 3/4/5, some Motorola g-series, Nothing (3)/(3a)/(4a), some Sony; not newer OnePlus or Xiaomi [S: https://github.com/chenxiaolong/avbroot/issues/299]. Samsung removed unlock in One UI 8 [S: https://www.sammobile.com/news/say-goodbye-to-your-custom-roms-as-one-ui-8-kills-bootloader-unlock/]; Xiaomi adds a 168-hour wait [S]. Eligible models come from 15. **[CORRECTED]** (a) The AVB README is a **LineageOS mirror** of `external/avb`, so [P] here means "real file, not Google's tree"; it documents `avb_custom_key` under "Pixel 2 and later" and defines yellow generically as "LOCKED state and the key used for verification was set by the end user". (b) avbroot issue 299 (readable only through a summarising fetch tool) lists working relock for Fairphone 3/4/5 (**not 6 or 6+**), Nothing (1)/(2)/(3)/(3a)/(4a), Sony Xperia 1 II/1 V/1 VI/5 IV/10 V, moto g32-g200, Edge S and ThinkPhone, Razer Phone 2 and OnePlus 6/6T; newer OnePlus and Nothing (2a) fail; Xiaomi "accepts but does not enforce" the key; Samsung is not in the issue. These are user reports on older firmware, not Android 17 tests of the phones we would qualify; only Pixel has a vendor-documented path (15). (c) Samsung: One UI 8 drops the OEM-unlock toggle per 9to5Google (2025-07-26) and Android Authority, reportedly in all regions (US models never had it); Samsung has not published a statement [S]. Xiaomi's 168-hour wait is confirmed [S]. (d) **Omitted bypass:** a relocked Snapdragon phone is only as tamper-resistant as its OEM bootloader. The March 2026 Qualcomm GBL exploit unlocked locked Android 16 flagships (Xiaomi 17, Redmi K90 Pro Max, POCO F8 Ultra) without the OEM-unlock toggle, and EDL firehose programmers leak from OEM repair channels (15) [S]. "OEM unlock off" therefore does not stop a determined child with a PC on those models; add each model's ABL/EDL status to qualification and keep the Pixel as the only pilot platform until it is known.

**F4. Warranty (all [S] search summaries; verify).** Google staff say unlocking alone does not void the Pixel warranty, but its text excludes "alterations" [S: https://support.google.com/store/answer/9155764]. Fairphone: intact if original Android is reinstalled before repair. Motorola, Sony, Nothing: unlocking reported to void it. Magnuson-Moss bars voiding cover merely for third-party service, but a warrantor may deny a defect the modification caused [S: https://www.taftlaw.com/news-events/law-bulletins/ftc-clarifies-that-a-manufacturer-provided-warranty-cannot-deceptively-imply-that-using-an-unauthorized-part-will-void-the-warranty/]. Every customer therefore needs a return-to-stock path. **[CORRECTED]** The Google, Taft and other cited pages were blocked; search summaries agree on each vendor: Google staff say unlocking does not void the Pixel warranty (flashing unofficial firmware may); Fairphone keeps the hardware warranty but wants the original OS reinstalled before repair; Motorola's unlock flow makes the customer accept voiding the warranty twice; a Nothing support executive has said unlocking voids it [S]. Magnuson-Moss: the manufacturer must show that the third-party service or modification caused the defect, but software modification is the least settled area because the Act does not mention it [S: natlawreview.com via search]. Treat "Pixel unlock is fine" as vendor policy, not law, and keep the return-to-stock promise.

**F5. Flash mechanics to copy** [P: GrapheneOS/device_common@17:generate-factory-images-common.sh]: fastboot version check; `getvar product` against the bundle ("wrong factory images... would likely brick"); both-slot bootloader; radio; `erase` then `flash avb_custom_key avb_pkmd.bin`; `fastboot -w update`. Platform-tools are pinned by SHA-256 and bundles verified by ed25519 signature [P: install/cli.html]. Flash takes 5-20 minutes [S]. **[CORRECTED]** Sequence reproduced [P: GrapheneOS/device_common@17:generate-factory-images-common.sh]: fastboot >= 35.0.1 check; `getvar product` must equal the bundle's device; bootloader flashed to the other slot, `--set-active=other`, `reboot-bootloader`, flashed again (both slots); radio; `fastboot erase avb_custom_key` then `fastboot flash avb_custom_key avb_pkmd.bin`; `fastboot -w --skip-reboot update image-*.zip`. Platform-tools r35.0.2 pinned by SHA-256 and ed25519 `ssh-keygen -Y verify` of the factory zip are on the install page [P: install/cli.html]; that signature scheme is GrapheneOS's own, so Zune must sign bundles with its own ed25519 key. **Two omitted gotchas [P]:** the generated script contains **no `fastboot -s` and no `ANDROID_SERIAL`**, so with four phones attached its bare `fastboot` calls fail or hit the wrong phone, and it **never runs `flashing unlock` or `flashing lock`** (the install page treats them as separate manual steps). The station wrapper must pin each phone by serial or USB device path (fastboot matches either [P: platform_system_core@17:fastboot.cpp `match_fastboot_with_serial`]) across the repeated `reboot-bootloader` cycles, and add the two confirmations itself. The 5-20 minute figure remains [S].

**F6. USB.** Hubs and cables "are the most common source of issues"; use direct ports [P: install/cli.html]. Parallel `fastboot` can hang; lock each call [S: https://bugs.launchpad.net/bugs/1110622]. Each model needs its own adapter (17). **[CORRECTED]** Install-page quote reproduced ("Many widely distributed USB cables and hubs are broken and are the most common source of issues"; avoid hubs and front panels, use rear ports; install from bare metal, not a VM) [P]. The launchpad.net page was blocked; a search summary identifies bug 1110622 as LAVA Dispatcher, January 2013, two Galaxy Nexus phones, fixed by wrapping `flash`/`boot` in `flock` [S]. That is a 13-year-old anecdote: sound design guidance, but prove it on the real cell with four current phones.

**F7. Key custody** [P: static/build.html]. `avb_pkmd.bin` "isn't needed for generating a signed release but rather to set the public key used by the device". RSA-4096 keys are scrypt/AES-256 encrypted at rest and decrypted to tmpfs only to sign; "unique keys per device variant"; keys "cannot be changed without flashing the generated factory images again which will perform a factory reset". **[CORRECTED]** Reproduced [P: static/build.html: `openssl genrsa 4096 | openssl pkcs8 -topk8 -scrypt -out avb.pem`, `avbtool.py extract_public_key`, "unique keys per device variant", scrypt + AES256 `encrypt-keys`, decrypt to tmpfs to sign]. Consequence the report does not draw: after hand-over OEM unlocking is off, and `avb_custom_key` is writable only while unlocked [P: AVB README], so changing the key on a fielded phone needs the parent-authorised service-unlock, a physical confirmation and a wipe **per phone**. Key rotation is a recall, not an OTA; the incident plan's "fleet re-flash" must be costed that way (and is a reason to keep per-model blast radius small).

**F8. After lock, the OS is the repair path.** Signed zips install through recovery sideload with downgrade protection [P: static/usage.html]. Returning to stock needs an unlocked bootloader and `fastboot erase avb_custom_key` [P: install/cli.html]. GrapheneOS keeps the unlock toggle; Zune removes it (04), so a service-unlock must exist [I]. **[CORRECTED]** Reproduced [P: static/usage.html "Sideloading": signed zips, downgrade protection; static/install/cli.html "Replacing GrapheneOS with the stock OS": unlocked bootloader, `fastboot erase avb_custom_key`]. These describe GrapheneOS's recovery; Zune's recovery is our own build, so sideload signature and downgrade checks must be tested on our image [I]. The AVB README adds that a locked custom-key phone still boots images signed by the **built-in** key [P], so the custom key adds trust, it does not remove Google's. The service-unlock constraint stated in section 6 is now checked against an Android 17 tree [P: GrapheneOS/platform_frameworks_base@17:services/core/java/com/android/server/oemlock/OemLockService.java, upstream code]: `setOemUnlockAllowedByUser` requires the `MANAGE_USER_OEM_UNLOCK_STATE` permission and an admin (or system) user, throws "OEM unlock disallowed while FRP is active" and "Admin does not allow OEM unlock" when `DISALLOW_FACTORY_RESET` is set, and throws if the carrier does not allow it. So the service-unlock order is: lift `DISALLOW_FACTORY_RESET` (parent-authorised), set the toggle, reboot to bootloader, then the physical `flashing unlock`.

**F9. Anti-rollback.** Pixel bootloader updates raise a rollback counter (Pixel 6/8 May 2025, Pixel 10 family May 2026); older images then cannot boot, and flashing one slot risks an unbootable fallback [S: https://www.droid-life.com/2025/05/06/pixel-6-and-pixel-8-devices-get-new-bootloader-in-may-update-that-wont-allow-rolling-back/]. A customer phone may be newer than our bundle, so the station blocks on `version-bootloader` before unlock (17 agrees). **[CORRECTED]** (a) The cited droid-life URL is the **May 2025** article (Pixel 6/8 families); it does not support "Pixel 10 family May 2026". That claim is nonetheless true: Google's May 2026 update raised the bootloader anti-rollback version on Pixel 10, 10 Pro, 10 Pro XL and 10 Pro Fold, and Google advises sideloading the full OTA after the first good boot so both A/B slots carry the new bootloader [S: droid-life.com/2026/05/05/google-issues-warning-for-may-2026-pixel-10-update/ and others, via search; page blocked]. **The Pixel 10a, the pilot model, is reported as excluded** [S], and no 2026 increment was found for the 9a (an XDA thread asks about one; unverified). (b) `version-bootloader` is a version string, not the rollback index. Search summaries name `fastboot getvar anti` (or `ap-ar-s`) as the Pixel anti-rollback variable [S, unverified]; the station should read whichever the model exposes and compare it with the bundle's index, not infer it from the string. (c) Android 17 reached Pixels on 2026-06-16 [S], so the "customer phone is newer than our bundle" case is live now: the bundle must track Google's monthly bootloader before cohort 1 ships, not after.

**F11. FRP and theft.** Google FRP lives in "a tiny, special region of persistent state not wiped by a factory reset" [P: faq.html]. An account-bound phone dead-ends at setup and we never bypass it. A flash service is also a laundering route for stolen phones: check the CTIA database and photo ID [S: https://cellularnews.com/mobile-phone/the-ctia-releases-the-stolen-phone-checker-tool-to-help-you-determine-your-phones-authenticity/]. **[CORRECTED]** FRP wording reproduced [P: static/faq.html "Does GrapheneOS provide Factory Reset Protection?": "a tiny, special region of persistent state not wiped by a factory reset"]. The CTIA Stolen Phone Checker (stolenphonechecker.org, powered by GSMA Device Check) is live [S].

## 3. Options & trade-offs

| Decision | Options | Pick |
|---|---|---|
| Intake | In person; mail-in; new-in-box drop-ship | In person for cohorts 1-2, drop-ship from cohort 2, mail-in in cohort 3 with a spare phone: no loaner, no shipping risk |
| Key custody | Fleet; per-model; per-device | Per-model (one model's blast radius); throwaway key only for staff-owned cohort 1 |
| Unlock bit after lock | On (recoverable) or off | Off: on lets a child with a PC unlock |
| OEM firmware | Fetch at flash time and pin digests in our vbmeta; or ship in our OTA | Fetch and pin for v1 (17 option b); OTA firmware needs OEM terms (17, G5) |

## 4. Recommended Stage-1 design

### 4.1 Customer journey

1. **Web form** (parent browser): model, SKU, carrier, screenshots of Settings > About and the OEM-unlocking toggle. Customers request any OEM unlock code (Fairphone) themselves; we never store it.
2. **Eligibility:** US unlocked SKU on 15's list; carrier-unlocked and paid off, with proof (F2); stock bootloader not newer than our bundle (F9); accounts removed, Settings reset, toggle available; CTIA check passed and photo ID matches (F11); battery not swollen, health >= 80%, no MDM; carrier verified by cohort QA (12).
3. **Agreement and consent online** before drop-off: wipe consent, backup duty, warranty and risk terms, parent account with COPPA consent (13).
4. **Drop-off:** ticket QR, tamper-evident bag, exterior photos only, customer confirms the reset in front of us. We never ask for a PIN. A locked or account-bound phone goes back unopened.
5. **Service** (4.2): 90-120 minutes; the parent waits or collects later.
6. **Hand-over:** the parent opens the portal on their own phone, taps "Add device", the child device scans the QR (05). The phone ships unenrolled, OEM unlock off. A 10-minute onboarding covers what the child sees, "ask a parent", SOS, and what parents see including SMS. Mail-in uses a claim card and a first-week call.
7. **After-sales:** one-business-day response; check-ins on days 3 and 14; free return-to-stock in pilot. **[CORRECTED]** Internal conflict: return-to-stock needs the service-unlock (F8), which section 4.5 builds only before cohort 2 (gate G1), yet cohort 1 is promised the same return path and the station turns OEM unlocking off at the end of factory mode. Either build a minimal service-unlock (lift `DISALLOW_FACTORY_RESET`, set the toggle, physical unlock) before cohort 1, or keep OEM unlocking on for cohort 1's staff-owned phones and say so in the QA table; the second option contradicts the "OEM unlocking off" attestation row and must be recorded as a cohort-1 exception.

### 4.2 Station and process

**BOM per 4-phone cell (about $3-5k):** bare-metal Ubuntu 24.04 workstation (16 GB RAM, 500 GB SSD) with PCIe USB host cards or direct ports; certified short USB-C cables with spares; a separate powered charging rack; lithium-safe cabinet; prepaid test SIMs per verified carrier; two reference Zune phones; lab AP; label printer.

**Software ("zune-station", a wrapper over pinned platform-tools, with one adapter per model, F6):**
1. Scan the ticket; read `product, serialno, version-bootloader, unlocked`; refuse on mismatch or rollback risk. **[CORRECTED]** Also read `flashing get_unlock_ability` (F1) and the model's anti-rollback variable (F9), and pin every later call to this phone's serial or USB path (F5).
2. Verify the bundle's ed25519 signature and SHA-256; serialise `fastboot` calls per device.
3. Human confirms unlock; flash firmware, `avb_custom_key`, OS, `-w` (F5).
4. Human confirms lock (wipes again); first boot into **factory mode**, a Zune system app, inert after setup: it runs QA, creates an attested key, and as the last step disables OEM unlocking.
5. Server verifies the attestation chain (`android/keyattestation`, 05): locked, our key hash [M]. Phone is left at the unenrolled welcome screen. **[CORRECTED] (the [M] tag is replaced by the evidence below; this is the report's weakest load-bearing claim as written).** *Reproduced [P]:* the verifier library's `RootOfTrust` carries `verifiedBootKey`, `deviceLocked`, `verifiedBootState` (enum includes `SELF_SIGNED(1)`) and an optional `verifiedBootHash` [P: android/keyattestation@55c3504 (2026-09-28) `src/main/kotlin/Extension.kt`]. GrapheneOS's Auditor rejects the attestation unless `deviceLocked`, then maps `verifiedBootKey` to a known device only when the state is `SELF_SIGNED` for a custom key (`VERIFIED` for stock) [P: GrapheneOS/Auditor@main `AttestationProtocol.java`]; the published "sha256 of the verified boot public key" shown on the yellow boot screen equals the keys in Auditor's table (Pixel 9a `0508DE44...`, Pixel 10a `D8F879D1...`) [P: grapheneos.org `templates/verified_boot_key_hash.html`], so the attested `verifiedBootKey` is the bootloader's own "sha256 of the verified boot public key" on Pixel; that this equals `sha256(avb_pkmd.bin)` is [I] (not computed here, since no GrapheneOS `avb_pkmd.bin` is public). The server must therefore check `deviceLocked == true` **and** `verifiedBootState == SELF_SIGNED` **and** the per-model key hash, plus patch levels. *Missing from the report [P/S]:* (a) **RKP dependency.** Devices launching with Android 16 support only remote key provisioning (the Pixel 10a, released March 2026, should be one [I]) [P: developer.android.com/privacy-and-security/security-key-attestation]; the RKP daemon takes its server from system property `remote_provisioning.hostname`, empty by default [P: GrapheneOS/platform_packages_modules_RemoteKeyProvisioning@17 `Settings.java`]; GrapheneOS fronts `remoteprovisioning.googleapis.com` with its own proxy and says a non-Google-certified OS "would need their own service" [P: grapheneos.org FAQ]. So Zune must ship rkpd, set the host, and let the station and every enrolling phone reach it (and not block it in the no-YouTube network policy); otherwise the QA gate fails for reasons unrelated to lock state. (b) **New root.** A new ECDSA P-384 root signs chains from 2026-02-01 (valid 2025-07-17 to 2035-07-15); trust both roots, accept the legacy root regardless of expiry, and use Google's Kotlin library, which handles expiry [P: same Google page]. (c) **Relay.** A challenge alone does not prevent relay [P: android/keyattestation README], and GrapheneOS says proxying to another device of the same model/OS/patch level can defeat the first verification until serial attestation exists [P: install/cli.html]; at a staffed station the risk is low, but a self-install gate (17, v2) must bind to station-recorded identity. (d) **Scope.** Standard attestation proves lock state, boot key and patch levels, not that the OEM-unlocking toggle is off (Auditor reads that through a GrapheneOS-specific extension); the factory-mode app must report it and sign with the attested key.
6. Print the certificate of erasure, bag, hand over.

**Identity and logs.** Random 128-bit job ID; log model, bundle ID and hash, key ID, bootloader versions, timestamps, return codes, QA results, attestation digest, technician, station. **No name, IMEI, serial, number or photo**; a job-scoped HMAC of the serial (90-day key) supports RMA. The raw IMEI goes only into the stolen check, then is dropped. Erasure certificate: two wipes plus `fastboot -w` [P], plus family-key crypto-shred on return (05).

**Keys.** Signing runs in the release pipeline on an HSM or air-gapped host with dual control: per-model AVB keys and a separate ed25519 bundle key. Every bundle is flashed, locked and booted on a canary of the same model before a station may pull it. The unlock bit stays on until the final factory-mode step, so a bad lock is recoverable (unlock, erase key, reflash) [I]. **Sanctioned unlock paths only: no exploit-based unlock (EDL, GBL), no FRP bypass** (17). **[CORRECTED] Factory mode is an unmodelled bypass surface.** A shipped "inert" system app that can create attested keys and switch OEM unlocking off is also, if re-entered, an app that could switch it on. "Inert after setup" [I] needs a one-way mechanism (a signed, single-use token consumed in protected storage, and removal from the image once cohort stations are retired), and the QA table needs a row proving factory mode cannot be re-entered after hand-over (boot reason, properties, intents, USB). Also unmodelled: between station and enrolment the phone sits unenrolled; for mail-in, whoever scans the claim QR first binds it, so bind the claim to the order's parent account at intake (05's single-use QR plus a ticket-to-account match).

**Unbrick.** Before lock: re-run the bundle. After handover: A/B rollback, recovery sideload (F8), service-unlock for reflash, else replacement or OEM RMA.

**Throughput (4-up, one technician):** 50-60 min hands-on, 90-120 wall (intake 8, stock prep 8, presses 4, flash 2 plus 12 unattended, QA with test SIM 15, hand-over 12): 8-10 phones a day, 15-20 once QA is scripted. 10 devices take a week part-time; 100 take 2.5 weeks for one technician; 1,000 take about 8 weeks with three technicians and three cells.
