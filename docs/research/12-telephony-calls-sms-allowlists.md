## 1. Summary and recommendation

Voice and SMS are buildable on Android 17 without Google apps, but not from AOSP parts alone. AOSP 17 ships the plumbing (Telecom, TeleService, TelephonyProvider, Mms service, CellBroadcast, carrier config) but no usable messaging app and no eSIM manager. On Pixels, IMS (VoLTE), the eSIM manager and carrier data come from Google's stock images. We must write: a Dialer/InCall app, an SMS "vault" app, a guardian/policy agent, and a patch set in Telecom and Telephony.

**Enforce the allowlist in the framework, not in apps.** Role-based hooks fail the "determined child" bar for three reasons found in the code: call screening cannot block outgoing calls, it fails open after 5 seconds, and `DISALLOW_OUTGOING_CALLS` is all-or-nothing and checked only at the intent entry point. Add a system-process policy service (signed policy, deny by default) consulted in-process by Telecom's incoming filter graph, by `TelephonyConnectionService` for outgoing and incoming PSTN/IMS connections, and by the MMI/USSD path. Emergency calls and parent numbers bypass it. The cost is a permanent fork of two modules, rebased every AOSP drop; a June 2026 Telecom CVE fix shows this surface is actively attacked. **[CORRECTED]** (a) "Consulted in-process" is true only for Telecom: its manifest sets `android:process="system"` (shares system_server), while Telephony is `com.android.phone` (uid phone, persistent) [P] Telecomm@17:AndroidManifest.xml, Telephony@17:AndroidManifest.xml. A check from `TelephonyConnectionService` or the phone layer into a system_server policy service is a Binder call (still synchronous and fail-closed, but not "no IPC"); the alternative is a signed snapshot file readable by both processes, which needs SELinux rules. (b) The fork is not just two source trees: with `RELEASE_TELECOM_MAINLINE_MODULE=true` and `RELEASE_TELEPHONY_MODULE=true` in the Android 17 `cp2a` release config, both ship in the `com.android.telephonycore` APEX (TelecomUi, TelecomServiceResources, framework-telecom, systemserver-classpath fragment) [P] GrapheneOS/platform_build_release@17:flag_values/cp2a, bp4a; platform_packages_modules_Telephony@17:apex/Android.bp. Our build signs that APEX with our own key and cannot rely on Google Play system updates, and code inside it may only call module-visible APIs, so the policy-service call surface needs an early prototype before the "1-2k lines" estimate is believed. (c) The CVE is CVE-2026-28615 (Telecomm permission bypass, published 2026-06-17, Android 17 devices at patch level 2026-07-01 or later are fixed) [S] search summaries of the Android Security Bulletin; the android-17.0.0_r1 tag is the June 2026 build and may predate it, so the base must include the fix (see risk 11).

SMS: hold the default SMS role with no UI. Persist each message to an encrypted queue, upload to the portal, wipe locally, never write the SMS provider, and grant READ_SMS, RECEIVE_SMS and SEND_SMS to no other app. Stage 1 does not capture MMS bodies and does not record call audio (wiretap exposure). Carrier path: US only, VoLTE only, Wi-Fi calling off, bring-your-own SIM in beta, then one host network (T-Mobile via an MVNE) at general availability.

### 2.2 Why role-based enforcement is not tamper-proof

- For outgoing calls Telecom binds the screening app only for caller-ID lookup and ignores the answer (`CallsManager.bindForOutgoingCallerId`, ~line 3300) [P]. **[CORRECTED]** The role-based mechanism that can gate outgoing calls is `CallRedirectionService.cancelCall()` (user-defined and carrier variants), which the report omits. It has the same weakness: Telecom waits only `user_defined_call_redirection_timeout` / `carrier_call_redirection_timeout` (5000 ms each) and then notifies the service of the timeout and proceeds, and it runs only on the intent path, which is where the 2026 bypasses were [P] Telecomm@17:callredirection/CallRedirectionProcessor.java, Timeouts.java. The conclusion (roles are not tamper-proof) stands.
- The incoming filter graph starts from `DEFAULT_RESULT` with `shouldAllowCall(true)`; on the default 5000 ms timeout it completes with that result (`IncomingCallFilterGraph`, `Timeouts.getCallScreeningTimeoutMillis`) [P]. A killed or slow app lets the call ring.
- `DISALLOW_OUTGOING_CALLS` is boolean, spares emergency numbers only, is checked in `UserCallIntentProcessor` and conference creation, and skips self-managed (VoIP) calls [P] Telecomm@17: UserUtil.java, components/UserCallIntentProcessor.java.
- Google's 2026-06-17 fix says unprivileged apps could bypass `NewOutgoingCallIntentBroadcaster` checks via the `UserCallActivity` trampoline and "silently execute dangerous MMI/USSD codes (e.g. call forwarding) or auto-dial emergency numbers" [P] Telecomm@17 commit 21585d3. **[CORRECTED/added]** Confirmed by reading the commit (title "Fix privilege escalation in ACTION_CALL intent trampoline", author at google.com, 2026-06-17). A second intent-layer MMI bypass was fixed three weeks earlier: commit 586e92c ("Fix MMI check bypass via leading whitespace in tel URI", 2026-05-29) [P]. Together with the CVE noted in section 1 this supports putting the authoritative check below the intent layer. It also means a base at the android-17.0.0_r1 tag, rather than a branch that includes these commits, would ship both bugs.
- `BlockedNumberContract` is a blocklist; an allowlist would mean importing the whole number space. The call-redirection role cannot stop emergency calls [P] NewOutgoingCallIntentBroadcaster.java header. **[CORRECTED]** The first sentence is wrong. The platform already has contacts-based inbound blocking: `BlockedNumberContract.SystemContract` defines enhanced settings `block_numbers_not_in_contacts_setting`, `block_private_number_calls_setting`, `block_payphone_calls_setting`, `block_unknown_calls_setting` and `block_unavailable_calls_setting`; Telecom's `BlockCheckerFilter` (first filter in the incoming graph) runs a contacts lookup and returns `setShouldAllowCall(false).setShouldReject(true)` with reason `BLOCK_REASON_NOT_IN_CONTACTS`; `CarrierConfigManager.KEY_SUPPORT_ENHANCED_CALL_BLOCKING_BOOL` defaults to true, and blocking is lifted for `KEY_DURATION_BLOCKING_DISABLED_AFTER_EMERGENCY_INT` (default 7200 s, two hours) after an emergency call [P] GrapheneOS/platform_frameworks_base@17:core/java/android/provider/BlockedNumberContract.java, telephony/java/android/telephony/CarrierConfigManager.java:11896,11975; Telecomm@17:callfiltering/BlockCheckerFilter.java. With contacts synced from the portal this is a ready-made inbound allowlist with no fork, usable as a Stage-0 prototype and as defence in depth, but it is just another filter in the same graph (provider IPC, 5 s fail-open timeout) and it does nothing for outgoing calls, so the framework hook is still needed. It also shows the AOSP precedent for an emergency-call exemption window, which is two hours in stock, not the 30 minutes proposed below.

### 2.3 Hooks already in the code

- `setUpCallFilterGraph` (CallsManager.java ~1100) is where a mandatory `ZuneAllowlistFilter` sits beside `BlockCheckerFilter` [P].
- `onSuccessfulIncomingCall` skips filtering for emergency-callback mode, network-identified emergency calls and emergency SMS mode; `Timeouts` defaults the callback window to 5 minutes [P]. **[CORRECTED/added]** The same branch also skips the whole graph for incoming self-managed (VoIP) calls unless `voipDndFocus` is on, and a PhoneAccount with `EXTRA_SKIP_CALL_FILTERING` gets only the DND filter [P] CallsManager.java:1044-1090. Any new filter must therefore be backed by checks in the connection service, and Zune's own VoIP calls (messenger, video) need their own device-side check (report 13 already makes this point). Also, a filter result only rejects when it sets both `shouldAllowCall=false` and `shouldReject=true` (`onCallFilteringComplete` rejects only inside the `!shouldAllowCall` branch; `combine()` ANDs allow and ORs reject), so `ZuneAllowlistFilter` must set both [P] CallsManager.java:1171-1300 (`onCallFilteringComplete`), CallFilteringResult.java:216-266.
- `TelephonyConnectionService.onCreateOutgoingConnection` (~line 1125) is the single PSTN/IMS choke point and already blocks call-forwarding prefixes per number while roaming [P] platform_packages_services_Telephony@17. **[CORRECTED]** It is a choke point, but not the lowest or the only precedent. `GsmCdmaPhone.dial` (frameworks/opt/telephony) already throws `CallStateException(ERROR_FDN_BLOCKED)` for non-emergency numbers when SIM Fixed Dialing Numbers are enabled, before the IMS-or-CS decision, so it covers both paths, and `FdnUtils`/`GsmCdmaPhone` also apply the FDN list to USSD and supplementary-service strings; `SmsController` applies it to outgoing SMS (`RESULT_ERROR_FDN_CHECK_FAILURE`); only STK calls can set `skipFdnCheck` [P] GrapheneOS/platform_frameworks_opt_telephony@17:src/java/com/android/internal/telephony/GsmCdmaPhone.java:1403-1409, 1609, 2345; FdnUtils.java; SmsController.java:208, 371, 463. The Telephony app also ships FDN settings UI (`settings/fdn/FdnSetting.java`) [P]. So (a) the cleanest fork point is an FDN-style, non-SIM check in `GsmCdmaPhone.dial` and `SmsController`, which also covers MMI/USSD and any caller of `Phone.dial`, with `TelephonyConnectionService` as a second layer; (b) FDN itself (PIN2-protected list on the SIM) is a free, hardware-backed Stage-2 defence in depth for outgoing calls and SMS if the carrier SIM supports it (support on eSIM and MVNE profiles is unverified [I]).
- `MmiUtils` already treats call-forwarding and vertical service codes (`*72`, `*67`...) as dangerous [P]. **[CORRECTED]** `MmiUtils` is in the Telecomm repo (`src/com/android/server/telecom/MmiUtils.java`), not Telephony. Its dangerous vertical-code set is `*09 *42 *56 *60 *63 *64 *68 *72 *77 *78`; plain `*67` (caller-ID block) and `*82` are not in it. `*67*<number>#` and `*21*<number>#` are caught only as GSM call-forwarding MMI strings (`SC_CFB`, `SC_CFU`). The loop that checks prefixes (`for prefixLen = max; prefixLen <= max`) only ever tests 3-character prefixes. This supports the report's own rule of rejecting every dial string containing `*`, `#` or letters rather than relying on `MmiUtils` [P].
- `ACTION_DIAL_EMERGENCY` resolves to `config_emergency_dialer_package`, so our dialer can own emergency UI [P] TelecomServiceImpl.java ~2721.
- "Secret codes" are broadcast by the Dialer app and merely consumed by Telecom's `DialerCodeReceiver` [P]; with no AOSP Dialer that surface disappears. TeleService test activities (RadioInfo, `*#*#4636#*#*`, which GrapheneOS uses for eSIM bring-up) must be stripped on `user` builds [P]/[I].

### 2.4 Caller-ID spoofing (STIR/SHAKEN)

`Call.Details.getCallerNumberVerificationStatus()` exists since API 30; on 4G+ only the verdict is shared [P] https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/telecom/dialer-app/prevent-spoofing. Telecom's `Call` carries it, so a system filter reads it directly [P]. Telephony sets it only from IMS call profiles (`ImsPhoneConnection.java:224`) and otherwise leaves "not verified" [P] frameworks_opt_telephony@17. So it exists on Android 17 but only on VoLTE/Wi-Fi calls where the carrier supplies it; whether the Pixel IMS stack populates it per US carrier is unverified [I].

### 2.5 SMS plumbing

- `SMS_DELIVER` goes only to the default SMS app; with none, to all receivers. Other RECEIVE_SMS holders get `SMS_RECEIVED` [P] InboundSmsHandler.java ~1915-1950, ~2199. We must hold the role.
- Role requires: SMS_DELIVER receiver, WAP_PUSH_DELIVER receiver, RESPOND_VIA_MESSAGE service, `smsto` SENDTO activity; `config_defaultSms` presets the holder [P] Permission@17:PermissionController/res/xml/roles.xml. Our SENDTO and respond-via stubs do nothing.
- Inbound PDUs sit in the provider `raw` table until the broadcast completes, so the vault must persist before returning [P]/[I]. SMS over IMS uses the same handler [P].
- Android 17 withholds OTP SMS from non-default apps for 3 hours; the default SMS app is exempt [P] https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17/behavior-changes-all. **[CORRECTED]** Narrower than stated: the delay covers messages in SMS Retriever format (since earlier releases) and, new in Android 17, WebOTP format, for apps that hold SMS read access but are not the intended recipient; during the delay `SMS_RECEIVED` is withheld and provider queries are filtered; exemptions are "certain apps such as the default SMS assistant app, connected device companion apps"; it applies at every target SDK. Ordinary OTP texts are not delayed. This does not affect the vault (default SMS app).
- No framework check blocks sending beyond permissions and app-ops (`DISALLOW_SMS` only affects broadcast routing and Telecom's reply-with-SMS) [P]. A2 is enforced by granting SEND_SMS to nobody. **[CORRECTED]** The first sentence is wrong for SIMs with FDN enabled: `SmsController` rejects sends (text, data, multipart) to non-FDN destinations with `RESULT_ERROR_FDN_CHECK_FAILURE` [P] frameworks_opt_telephony@17:SmsController.java:208, 371, 463. The `DISALLOW_SMS` half is confirmed: in frameworks/opt/telephony it appears only in `InboundSmsHandler` (line 1506) [P, that repo only]. A2 enforced by "SEND_SMS to nobody" is still sound for apps, but an SMS-send hook in `SmsController` (the framework-level counterpart of the call hook) is the tamper-resistant version, and is needed anyway if A2 flips to "child may text allowlisted numbers".

### 2.8 Law and regulation (all need counsel)

- 911: carriers must deliver all wireless 911 calls regardless of validation, including non-initialized handsets [S] https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/9.10. WEA devices must keep alerts in a consumer-accessible place for 24 hours [S] https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-10. **[CORRECTED]** Pin-cites (from search summaries; the Cornell and eCFR pages were egress-blocked): the "all calls" rule is 47 CFR 9.10(b) ("without respect to their call validation process"); the 24-hour WEA preservation rule is 47 CFR 10.500(h) (added by the January 2018 WEA order, effective for devices after 2019-11-30). "Including non-initialized handsets" is UNVERIFIED: the FCC proposed sunsetting the non-service-initialized 911 obligation in 2015 and I found no final order either way. These duties bind carriers and the WEA-capable device; the report does not say who is the regulated party for a Zune handset (see risk 10).
- Wiretap Act is one-party consent; parental "vicarious consent" is accepted by some courts, rejected by others; all-party states complicate it; access after receipt is generally outside "interception", but forwarding from the device is a gray area [S] https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R41733, https://privacyresearchgroup.law.nyu.edu/2016/04/parental-eavesdropping-an-exception-for-best-interests/. **[CORRECTED]** CRS R41733 is the 2012 general overview "Privacy: An Overview of the Electronic Communications Privacy Act"; it supports the one-party-consent and stored-communications framing but is not the authority for vicarious consent. The on-point case is `Pollock v. Pollock` (6th Cir. 1998), which adopted vicarious consent where the parent has a good-faith, objectively reasonable belief that recording is in the child's best interest, and noted that other courts disagree [S] findlaw/casemine via search. Neither covers an SMS vault that forwards the child device's received texts to a portal. Add for counsel [M]: pen-register/trap-and-trace rules for call metadata (18 U.S.C. 3121 et seq., consent exception), Stored Communications Act exposure and legal-process handling for the portal's SMS archive, state all-party-consent statutes (California Penal Code 631/632/632.7 is the usual example), and the other party to every call or text never consenting.
- COPPA amendments: effective 2025-06-23, compliance by 2026-04-22; separate consent for third-party disclosure; written retention policy [S] https://www.dglaw.com/the-ftc-finalizes-amendments-to-the-coppa-rule/. **[CORRECTED]** Dates confirmed by independent search summaries [S]; the amended rule also requires a written information-security program (annual risk assessment, vendor due diligence) [S], which the report omits and report 13 already flags.
- Third-party senders and callers cannot be notified; GDPR/ePrivacy only if EU launch [M].

## 4. Recommended Stage-1 design

**Components**
1. `ZunePhone` (DIALER role): contacts-only call list, large emergency button, `ACTION_DIAL`, `ACTION_DIAL_EMERGENCY`, `InCallService`; no free keypad (an "ask a parent to add a number" box feeds the portal); no voicemail, add-call or secret codes.
2. `ZuneSmsVault` (SMS role, no launcher, no notifications): SMS_DELIVER and WAP_PUSH_DELIVER receivers plus the two stubs. `onReceive` writes to an encrypted queue (Keystore AES-GCM); WorkManager uploads over TLS 1.3 with device-signed requests; on ack it deletes and checkpoints. Queue cap: 14 days or 5,000 messages, then drop oldest with a gap marker. Never writes `content://sms`.
3. `ZuneGuardian` (privileged): pairing, policy sync, event upload, own push channel (no FCM; reconcile with report 05).
4. `ZunePolicyService` (system service, `/data/system/zune/policy.bin`): verifies an ECDSA P-256 portal signature with pinned key, monotonic version and expiry; exposes `check(direction, e164, presentation, verstat, now)` to Telecom and Telephony; deny-by-default if file missing or invalid. **[CORRECTED]** Telecom (process `system`) can call it directly; Telephony (`com.android.phone`) reaches it over Binder, so the Telephony-side call needs a short timeout that fails closed (reject, not allow) and a startup-ordering rule (phone process may start before the service is registered). Section 3's "in-process snapshot; no IPC on the call path" is therefore only true for Telecom. Both sides now live in the `com.android.telephonycore` APEX (section 2.1), so the service API must be reachable from module code (system-server-visible or a stable AIDL), which should be prototyped first.

**Decision function**
1. Emergency: `TelephonyManager.isEmergencyNumber` plus explicit 911, 112, 988 (988 is an ordinary routed number [I]) always allowed.
2. Inbound in emergency-callback state or window: allow everything, including withheld numbers, using AOSP's skip path, extended to 30 minutes after any emergency call [I; confirm with carrier and counsel]. **[CORRECTED]** This is a bypass as written: AOSP's skip is for `PROPERTY_EMERGENCY_CALLBACK_MODE` and `PROPERTY_NETWORK_IDENTIFIED_EMERGENCY_CALL`, with a 5-minute `emergency_callback_window_millis` default, and the stock blocker uses a 2-hour carrier-config window [P]. A child (or a friend arranging it) can place a real 911 call and then receive any number's calls unfiltered for the whole window; 30 minutes is longer than the 5-minute AOSP default and no source supports it. Prefer: honour only AOSP's own flags (no extension), allow withheld or unknown callers only inside the window and only while the emergency call's callback flag is set, log it, and raise an immediate portal alert "emergency call placed, filter relaxed until HH:MM". Hoax 911 calls are an offence in most US states [M], so say so in child-facing text.
3. Guardian numbers: allowed both ways, immune to schedules; inbound rejected only if verstat is FAILED.
4. Everyone else: exact E.164 match (libphonenumber, region from SIM, post-dial digits stripped), matching direction and schedule window. Withheld, alphanumeric, SIP and short-code numbers are never allowed unless added. Any dial string with `*`, `#` or letters is rejected, which blocks all MMI/USSD. No suffix matching.
5. Policy missing or unsynced beyond 14 days: guardians and emergency only. Never fail open.
6. Time: last signed server time plus `elapsedRealtime`; `DISALLOW_CONFIG_DATE_TIME`.

**Hook points (about 1-2k lines [I])**: `UserCallIntentProcessor` (friendly "ask your parent" dialog), `TelephonyConnectionService.onCreateOutgoingConnection` (authoritative), `CallsManager.startOutgoingCall`, mandatory `ZuneAllowlistFilter` in `setUpCallFilterGraph` (reject always wins), MMI/USSD in `handleMmiCode` and the phone layer, ignore `EXTRA_SKIP_CALL_FILTERING` on the PSTN account. Blocked inbound calls are rejected, logged and raised as "pending approval". **[CORRECTED]** Add the lowest-level hooks that AOSP already uses for FDN: `GsmCdmaPhone.dial` (both IMS and CS, plus USSD and supplementary-service strings) and `SmsController` (all three send paths), with the same emergency exemption and no `skipFdnCheck` for non-STK callers (section 2.3). The `ZuneAllowlistFilter` result must set `shouldAllowCall=false` and `shouldReject=true`. Because `CallsManager.startOutgoingCall` is also reached from Bluetooth HFP, TelecomManager.placeCall and system callers, hooks above `onCreateOutgoingConnection` are convenience only.

**Bypass matrix**

| Vector | Control |
|---|---|
| `tel:`/ACTION_CALL from other apps | No third-party apps; enforcement below the intent layer |
| MMI/USSD, `*21*`, `*67`, `*#06#`, hidden codes | Dial-string rule; no Dialer; strip TeleService test activities; no Stk, so SIM "set up call" has no handler [I] |

| Second SIM, SIM swap | Policy is SIM-independent; SIM lock is Stage 2 |

| Debug, reset | `user` build, `DISALLOW_DEBUGGING_FEATURES`, factory reset and safe boot disallowed (reports 04, 10) |

**SMS hygiene.** Nothing but the vault touches SMS text: no notifications, no Settings entry, no READ_SMS/RECEIVE_SMS for the Assistant (report 07) or anything else (privapp allowlist), `allowBackup=false` with empty extraction rules, no content logging, adb off. Consequences of A2: no text-to-911, no "STOP" replies, no quick-reply texts; any carrier flow needing a mobile-originated SMS must be tested with the host carrier. Short codes and OTPs are captured and flagged; parent push notifications never carry bodies.

**Carrier setup.** US only; VoLTE on; VoWi-Fi, VVM, RCS and voicemail off via CarrierConfig2 overrides [I]. CellBroadcastReceiver stays visible with alert history (24-hour rule). eSIM profiles are downloaded by a privileged Zune Setup app through `EuiccManager` and the stock LPA [I], which inherits report 02's Pixel-binary licensing question.
