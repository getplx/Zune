Summary
Zune · Investor brief · Draft 2 Oct 2026

A phone where the unsafe things simply do not exist

What we are building, why it can win, what could kill it

What it is

A custom Android 17 (AOSP) OS image for children plus a parent web portal. The child's phone has no web browser, no route to YouTube, no cellular calls or SMS. It has WhatsApp-style 1:1 messaging, voice and video calls with parent-approved contacts, a kid-safe AI assistant (text + images), curated learning videos, weather with lessons, walkie-talkie, camera, photos, journal, notebook and a book reader.

Sold as software plus a flash-and-hand-over service on the customer's own qualified phone. Version 1: we flash and re-lock the phone and hand it back (about 200 users, India, Pixel 10a/9a). Later: customer self-install, then our own hardware.

The bet

Parents should not have to police a general-purpose phone. Remove the dangerous capabilities from the OS, and give parents a portal for everything that remains.

Why it can win

  • Enforcement in the image, not an app. No browser and no YouTube route exist on the device; the bootloader is re-locked so only our signed images boot.
  • Contact graph, not phone numbers. 1:1 text, voice, video and walkie-talkie only with contacts both families approve; no groups, no strangers.
  • One product, not ten apps. AI tutor, curated learning videos, weather lessons, camera, journal, book reader, parent portal.
  • Software-led model. No hardware inventory; recurring revenue from cloud services (portal, AI, comms, content).
  • Timing. Android 17 ships a native supervision framework; child-safety law is tightening worldwide.

What could kill it

  • Google firmware licence for commercially re-distributing Pixel firmware is unread and unresolved. High
  • Security patching. AOSP source drops twice a year; public fixes reach downstream months late (~125-day median seen). High
  • Re-lock support. Only Pixel has vendor-documented custom-key re-lock; Snapdragon phones are unproven. High
  • India child-privacy law (DPDP). Under-18 is a child; a parental-monitoring product needs legal confirmation. High
  • Addressable base. Qualified Pixels are scarce and costly in India (see Market). Med
  • YouTube API terms for topic-driven curation inside a closed client. Med
Details, mitigations and evidence quality: Risk register and Evidence status pages.

Problem

Every kids' phone today is policy on top of an open phone

Where Zune sits, and why now
1. Screen-time and filter apps
Family Link, Qustodio, Net Nanny, Bark app
  • Policy layer on an open phone
  • Child can still reach a browser and YouTube
  • Defeated by reset, safe mode, sideloading, other apps
  • Parents police an adult-grade device
2. Managed kids' phones
Pinwheel, Bark Phone, Gabb, Troomi
  • Rebadged commodity phones plus managed software
  • Browser-free by policy, not by image (our reading; to validate)
  • Subscription $15-20/mo; devices $100-240 (secondary sources)
  • Pinwheel does not install on your own phone
3. Zune
Capabilities are absent from the OS image
  • No browser or YouTube exit in the image; re-locked bootloader
  • Contact graph both families approve; no strangers, no groups
  • Parent portal in any browser; signed policy; offline-safe
  • Software-led: customer brings the phone

Why now

Safety defaults

  • Every contact needs parent approval; cross-family contacts need both families
  • No groups, links, attachments or discovery in messaging (Stage 1)
  • No recording or covert listening on calls or walkie-talkie
  • Stage 1 locations: parent-set places only

Stage 2: make it better

  • Richer content, games, sensors, climate stories
  • Parent-side voice and video calling
  • Customer self-install and more qualified devices
  • More languages (Hindi and regional)

Parent portal (any browser)

  • Approve contacts (both families for cross-family)
  • Read messages and AI chats; review calls metadata
  • Set screen-time and quiet hours
  • Control AI and learning-video settings
  • Pair and manage devices (QR claim code)
  • Remote lock; activity feed; approvals queue
  • Later: parent-side voice and video calling

Principles

  • English only; India first
  • Parent-visible by design (age-appropriate disclosure to the child)
  • Safe when the network is down (signed, cached policy)
  • Nothing the child does can add an app or open the web

Roadmap
Zune · Investor brief · Draft 2 Oct 2026

Roadmap: gates before dates

Sequenced by dependency; durations come after staffing and funding decisions

Gates

  • G0 Legal: written answer or counsel opinion on Google firmware; Indian counsel on DPDP and OTT rules; no paid launch before it.
  • G1 Device: re-lock verified on a sacrificial unit of every qualified model; OEM terms signed.
  • G2 Security: monthly patch pipeline, key custody (offline HSM), OTA tested on real devices.
  • G3 Pilot exit: install success, support load, child-safety incidents and bypass attempts within thresholds.

External dependencies

  • Google: Pixel firmware licence; partner security-patch access (unknown for a startup)
  • Indian counsel: DPDP consent architecture; OTT messaging; handset rules
  • LLM vendor: terms for products used by children; India availability
  • YouTube: API compliance review for a closed, curated client
  • Hosting: India-region cloud; LiveKit (self-host or cloud)
No calendar dates are stated because no staffing or funding assumptions exist yet. Phases derive from reports R01, R02, R15, R16, R17, R18, R21.
