Summary
Zune · Investor brief · Draft 3 Oct 2026

A phone where the unsafe things simply do not exist

What we are building, why it can win, what could kill it

What it is

A custom Android 17 (AOSP) OS image for children plus a parent web portal. The child's phone has no web browser, no route to YouTube, no cellular calls or SMS. It has WhatsApp-style 1:1 messaging, voice and video calls with parent-approved contacts, a kid-safe AI assistant (text + images), curated learning videos, weather with lessons, walkie-talkie, camera, photos, journal, notebook and a book reader.

Sold as software plus a flash-and-hand-over service on the customer's own qualified phone. Version 1: we flash and re-lock the phone and hand it back (about 200 users, India, Pixel 10a/9a). Later: customer self-install, then our own hardware.

The bet

Parents should not have to police a general-purpose phone. Remove the dangerous capabilities from the OS, and give parents a portal for everything that remains.

Why it can win

  • Enforcement in the image, not an app. No browser and no YouTube route exist on the device; the bootloader is re-locked so only our signed images boot.
  • Contact graph, not phone numbers. 1:1 text, voice, video and walkie-talkie only with contacts both families approve; no groups, no strangers.
  • The moat is the whole product, not the lock. Kid-only contact graph, AI tutor, curated learning content and portal. A no-browser Pixel is already sold by Pinwheel (US) and a $199 phone by Freckle.
  • Software-led model. No hardware inventory; recurring revenue from cloud services (portal, AI, comms, content).
  • Timing. Android 17 ships a native supervision framework; child-safety law is tightening worldwide.

What could kill it

  • Google firmware licence for commercially re-distributing Pixel firmware is unread and unresolved. High
  • Security patching. AOSP source drops twice a year; public fixes reach downstream months late (~127-day median seen). High
  • India child-privacy law (DPDP s.9(3)). Under-18 is a child; parent-visible monitoring has no clear exemption. Counsel first. High
  • Re-lock support. Only Pixel has vendor-documented custom-key re-lock; Snapdragon phones are unproven. High
  • Willingness to pay in India. ~Rs 1,250/month proposed vs JioShield at Rs 1,000/year; ~40,000 parents must be reached to sign ~200. High
  • Killable dependencies. YouTube must be optional (Videos work without it); the LLM vendor's approval for a child-facing product is gate one. Med

No browser
Zune · Investor brief · Draft 3 Oct 2026

No-browser is enforced in the locked image, not by deleting an app

No-browser lockdown: threat model and enforcement
R04unverified

Decision

Compile 'no browser' into a locked, verified image: user build, Zune AVB key, WebView caller gate, no http(s) handler, INTERNET allowlist. Policy only tunes it.

Why it matters

The product's core promise depends on it. Real kid-control bypasses are hidden-browser bugs, so the defensible claim is narrow: no browser app, no way to type a URL.

Key points

  • AOSP 17 is not browser-free: CarrierDefaultApp (disabled http/https wildcard handler, JS WebView), OsuLogin and HTMLViewer are escape hatches.R04 F1
  • AOSP 17 ships no WebView implementation. Vanadium (hardened Chromium, GPL-2.0 patches) or an own Chromium build means owning a Chromium update pipeline.R04 F2
  • WebView gate: system_server already sees caller UID, so a ~20-line allowlist restricts WebView to two Zune packages (feasibility inferred).R04 F3
  • App-level origin allowlists leak: shouldInterceptRequest misses redirects, blob:, javascript: and assets; Network Security Config has no allowlist.R04 F4
  • eBPF denies INTERNET at socket creation per app. Stage 1 grants it to about 8 packages; the EPUB reader gets none.R04 F6

Zune · Investor brief · Draft 3 Oct 2026

Google's parental controls aren't in AOSP, so Zune builds engine, backend and portal.

Parental-controls platform: portal, on-device enforcement, sync
R05unverified

Decision

Build ZuneGuardian, a system app that is Device Owner and holds the SYSTEM_SUPERVISION role, enforcing signed cloud policy locally. Device Owner is a one-way door awaiting founder approval.

Why it matters

Parent control is the core promise. With no Google services there is no Family Link, so control plane, tamper resistance and offline behaviour must be built in-house.

Key points

  • Cloud is authority; device enforces signed JWS ES256 bundles locally. Past expiry plus 14-day grace, it falls to guardians and emergency only.R05 4.4
  • Sync is one persistent TLS WebSocket as a doorbell (policy.update, then HTTPS GET); polling and alarms as fallback. No FCM, no UnifiedPush.R05 4.5
